Job Market Paper

Robust Alignment of Incentives

Paper

I study robust regulation of a better-informed agent whose incentives are imperfectly aligned with the principal’s. The principal faces ambiguity about both the consequences of the agent’s actions and his private bias, but knows that small changes in the action cannot have arbitrarily large payoff consequences. She may impose action-contingent penalties or restrict the set of actions available to the agent. I characterize corrective interventions that minimize worst-case regret. Their form depends on the principal’s information about the agent’s bias. If she can bound its overall magnitude but not its local variation, the optimal intervention is a staircase tax. If she instead knows bounds on the marginal bias, it is a kinked piecewise-linear tax.

Working Papers

Persuasion in Lemons Markets

with Jose Higueras
Paper

We study information disclosure in competitive markets with adverse selection. Sellers privately observe product quality, with higher quality entailing higher production costs, while buyers trade at the market-clearing price after observing a public signal. Because sellers’ participation in trade conveys information about quality, the designer faces endogenous constraints in the set of posteriors that she can induce. We reformulate the designer’s problem as a martingale optimal transport exercise with an additional condition that rules out further information transmission through sellers’ participation decisions, and characterize the optimal signals. When the designer maximizes trade volume, the solution features negative-assortative matching of inefficient and efficient sellers. When the objective is a weighted combination of price and surplus, optimal signals preserve this structure as long as the weight on the price is high enough, otherwise they fully reveal low-quality types while pooling middle types with high-quality sellers.

Price Impact of Insurance

with Jose Higueras
Paper

This paper analyzes optimal insurance design when the insurer internalizes the effect of coverage on third-party service prices. A monopolistic insurer contracts with risk-averse agents who have sequential two-dimensional private information and preferences represented by Yaari’s dual utility. Insurance contracts shape service demand and, through a market-clearing condition, determine equilibrium third-party prices. We show that contracts take simple forms —either full coverage after a deductible is paid or limited coverage with an out-of-pocket maximum— closely mirroring real-world insurance plans. Technically, we formulate the problem as a sequential screening model and solve it using tools from optimal transport theory.

Fertility-Based Discrimination

with Giacomo Marcolin
Paper

We study employer discrimination based on expectations about women’s future fertility. The identification challenge is that expectations about fertility cannot be varied independently of the characteristics from which employers infer them. We show that discrimination can be identified from variation in employers’ cost of future fertility, without requiring variation in fertility expectations. We exploit the Pregnancy Discrimination Act of 1978, which first provided federal employment protection to pregnant workers, raising firms’ expected costs of employing women more likely to become pregnant. Using a difference-in-differences design comparing newly affected states with states that already had comparable protections, we find that the PDA reduced employment among women of childbearing age by 6.6%, primarily through lower hiring. The decline is concentrated among women with higher predicted future fertility, while employment of men and older women is largely unaffected. Our estimates imply that expected-fertility discrimination accounts for at least 27% of the observed gender employment gap.

Work in Progress

Selling Information

with Jose Higueras

We study the sale of information to a buyer who is privately endowed with an existing experiment. Before the buyer's experiment realizes, the seller can offer additional correlated information. The value of this information depends both on the buyer's endowment and on the decision problem in which it is used. We ask whether the seller needs to know the buyer's decision problem to design its information products. We show that, with two states and a finite number of types ordered by strong Blackwell order, introduced by Brooks, Frankel, and Kamenica (2024), full surplus extraction is always possible through a menu of experiments that is independent of the decision problem. We characterize such experiments. To establish this result, for any two experiments π and π′, we introduce and characterize π′-minimal completions of π: experiments that fully reveal the state when combined with π while being least informative in the Blackwell order when combined with π′. We also establish necessary and sufficient conditions for the existence of an uninformative completion, which fully informs one type without providing any additional information to another.
